## Master Index

|Concept|Description|
|---|---|
|[[Acid House]]|Late-1980s UK mutation of Chicago house that sparked the British rave explosion|
|[[Afrodiasporic Music]]|Musical traditions rooted in the African diaspora, foundational to most bass-heavy genres|
|[[Bass and Identity]]|Scholarly area examining how bass culture intersects with race, gender, class, and resistance|
|[[Bass and the Body]]|Study of how low frequencies affect movement, sensation, dance, pain, and spirituality|
|[[Bass Culture]]|Broad term for musical and cultural traditions centered on low-frequency sound; also a UK research project (2016–19)|
|[[Bass Space and Place]]|Examination of how bass music shapes and is shaped by specific environments|
|[[BPM (Beats Per Minute)]]|Measure of tempo; a key variable distinguishing rave genres from one another|
|[[Breakbeats]]|Syncopated, non-four-on-the-floor drum patterns central to [[Drum and Bass]] and related genres|
|[[Dancehall]]|Jamaican genre descended from reggae; a key branch of sound system culture|
|[[DJ Culture]]|The practice and artistry of mixing records, central to all rave and sound system traditions|
|[[The Drop]]|The moment of rhythmic/melodic release in a track; a studied cultural and musical phenomenon|
|[[Drum and Bass]]|Fast UK genre using breakbeats and heavy bass; deeply tied to rave history|
|[[Dub-Reggae]]|Jamaican studio genre emphasizing bass and echo; foundational to global sound system culture|
|[[Four-on-the-Floor]]|Kick drum on every beat; the rhythmic backbone of house and techno|
|[[Genre as Tool]]|The idea that genre labels are practical guides rather than fixed categories|
|[[Goa Trance]]|Early form of psytrance originating from Goa party scenes in the 1980s–90s|
|[[Hard Techno]]|High-BPM, aggressive techno variant emphasizing physical intensity|
|[[Hardcore]]|High-energy rave genre distinct from hard techno and hardstyle despite surface similarities|
|[[Hardcore Continuum]]|Simon Reynolds's theoretical framework tracing a lineage of UK bass-driven club music|
|[[Hardstyle]]|Dutch-origin hard dance genre; often confused with hardcore|
|[[Hip-Hop]]|Afrodiasporic genre embedding dub-reggae practices; named in Source 1 as a key bass culture form|
|[[House]]|Chicago-born groove-oriented genre rooted in disco and soul; foundational to rave|
|[[Industrial / EBM]]|European 1980s genres using mechanical rhythms; influential on modern techno aesthetics|
|[[Jungle / Drum and Bass]]|UK genre combining fast breakbeats with reggae bass; central to the [[Hardcore Continuum]]|
|[[Miami Bass]]|US bass music subculture emphasizing low-end; cited as an active sound system scene|
|[[Musicology of Bass]]|Emerging scholarly call to develop formal academic study of bass culture|
|[[Psytrance]]|Hypnotic, fast trance variant focused on continuous motion and altered states|
|[[Rave Culture]]|The broader social and subcultural world surrounding rave music events|
|[[Reggae]]|Jamaican genre and predecessor to dub; central to Afrodiasporic sound system traditions|
|[[Sonic Epistemology]]|Theory of how knowledge is produced and transmitted through sound and listening|
|[[Sonic Materialism]]|Philosophical approach treating sound as physical matter with real effects on bodies|
|[[Sonic Street Technologies]]|Research project (2021–25) studying diasporic sound system cultures in the Global South|
|[[Sound System Culture]]|The tradition of large-scale speaker systems used for communal music events, rooted in Jamaica|
|[[Techno]]|Detroit-born, rhythm-first electronic genre; backbone of global rave culture|
|[[Trance]]|Western European genre combining hypnotic repetition with emotional melodic peaks|
|[[Treble Culture]]|Counterpart concept to bass culture; used to theorize divergent sonic aesthetics|
|[[Vinyl and Dubplates]]|Analogue media technologies central to sound system and DJ culture|
