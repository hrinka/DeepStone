[HOOK]
You call a number. You get a postcode. At 1am, you drive into darkness.

[CORE]
That's how a rave worked. No address. No guest list. No VIP.

The music came from Chicago — from Black and gay communities in the late seventies who built spaces where they couldn't be touched. That sound crossed the Atlantic. When it hit British warehouse speakers, the bass moved your clothes.

Frankie Bones gave it a code: Peace, Love, Unity. His version: "peace, love, and unity... I'll break your faces." It was a rule. A refusal.

Rave was a place that wasn't supposed to exist.

[TRACK]
The sound of this era: Larry Heard — "Can You Feel It" (1986). Made for a back room in Chicago. Music that doesn't need to look at you.

## Director Notes

- **HOOK**: Flat delivery. No drama. State it like you're already in the car. The image does the work.
- **CORE beat 1** ("No address. No guest list. No VIP."): Each phrase lands separately. Brief pause between them.
- **CORE beat 2** (Chicago roots): Slower here. This is weight, not pace. "couldn't be touched" — let it sit a half-beat longer.
- **"the bass moved your clothes"**: No added emphasis. Say it plainly. That's what makes it land.
- **Frankie Bones quote**: Deliver the full quote as one continuous line, then pause before "It was a rule." The register shift is the point.
- **Final CORE line** ("a place that wasn't supposed to exist"): Quieter than everything before it. This is the thread closing, not a punchline.
- **TRACK**: Warm. Unhurried. A recommendation from someone who loves the record.
