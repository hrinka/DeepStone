## Key Facts Established

### The Ibiza Origin (Summer 1987)
- Four London DJs took a holiday to Ibiza: **Danny Rampling, Paul Oakenfold, Nicky Holloway, Johnny Walker**
- They discovered **Amnesia** — an open-air club
- First encounter with MDMA (ecstasy) + acid house music in that setting
- Experience described as transformative — connected strangers, no aggression, total communal joy
- They returned to London with the intention to replicate this feeling

### London Club Era (Late 1987 — Mid 1988)
- **Shoom** (Danny Rampling): November 1987, basement of a Fitness Centre gym in Southwark, South London. Capacity ~240. Birthplace of UK acid house.
- **Future** (Paul Oakenfold)
- **Spectrum** (Paul Oakenfold + Ian St Paul): later moved to Heaven nightclub, Charing Cross
- **Trip** (Nicky Holloway)
- **The Haçienda**, Manchester (Mike Pickering + Graeme Park): Northern UK equivalent
- Pirate radio: Kiss FM, Centreforce spread the word

### The Smiley Face Symbol
- Emerged from a flyer for the third Shoom party
- Became ubiquitous: t-shirts, badges, flyers
- Synonymous with the era globally
- The Sun newspaper sold smiley face t-shirts — then called the scene evil a week later

### From Clubs to Fields (Late 1988 — 1989)
- **Tony Colston-Hayter**: disenchanted with Shoom's strict door policy, started his own events
- First called "Apocalypse Now," then renamed **Sunrise**
- November 1988, Guy Fawkes Night: Sunrise party at an abandoned gas works
  - 4,000 tickets sold
  - Riot police raided
  - By 5am, police withdrew — outnumbered
- Events spread: Blackburn, Nottingham, across all UK
- M25 orbital motorway became the axis for field raves
  - Promoters: Biology, Energy, Genesis, Sunrise, Back to the Future, Weekend World
  - The recently completed M25 provided easy countryside access from London
  - Band **Orbital** (Phil + Paul Hartnoll) named themselves after the M25

### Crowd Sizes
- Late summer 1988: "very large numbers of distinct gatherings held weekly"
- 24 June 1989: newspaper headline — "11,000 youngsters go drug crazy at Britain's biggest-ever Acid party"
- By 1989: parties routinely drew tens of thousands; clogged motorway junctions

### Police vs Ravers
- Police roadblocks on M25
- Ravers used pirate radio stations to broadcast alternative routes in real time
- Ravers consistently outnumbered and outmaneuvered police
- Led directly to the Criminal Justice Act 1994 (EP-08 topic)

### Media Panic
- **12 October 1988**: The Sun ran acid house as "cool and groovy" + sold smiley t-shirts
- **19 October 1988**: The Sun ran "Evils of Ecstasy" — 180-degree reversal in one week
- Mainstream media began "moral panic" coverage through late 1988

### Music Characteristics
- Roland TB-303 "squelching" bass synthesizer
- Repetitive four-on-the-floor beats, ~130 BPM
- Chicago acid house origin, transformed in UK context
- Merged with UK rave energy and MDMA culture

### Cultural Unifier
- "Gay, straight, black or white; plumbers, barristers, shop workers, doctors, football hooligans… all came together"
- Class and race boundaries dissolved on the dancefloor
- Deliberate echo of San Francisco's 1967 Summer of Love

## Content Angle for Charlotte R1nR1n
The story of 4 people who had an experience so powerful they couldn't keep it to themselves. The club scene was just the beginning — the real story is what happens when a culture outgrows the walls that contain it. The cat-and-mouse with police. The radio-as-weapon. The newspaper that sold the smiley, then called it evil 7 days later. The Second Summer of Love is the story of what happens when something is truly unstoppable.
